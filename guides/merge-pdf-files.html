How to Merge PDF Files for Free

Combine several PDFs into one tidy file in under a minute. Free, in your browser, with no signup and no watermark.

Three separate PDFs for one application. A certificate in one file, a transcript in another, a letter in a third — and the form you are submitting to accepts only one upload. Merging them is a five-minute job once you know how, and you do not need paid software, an account, or an app install to do it.

This guide shows you how to combine PDF files into a single document for free, in your browser. The pages stay in the order you choose, nothing is re-typed, and the result downloads as one clean file.

What you need

  • The PDF files you want to combine.
  • A modern browser — Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari.
  • One minute, and a clear idea of the order the pages should be in.

Before you start, check that every file actually opens. A damaged PDF will not merge, and it is better to find that out before you arrange twenty pages. Also decide the order now: for an application, the usual order is application form first, then certificates, then supporting letters.

Step 1: Open the free merge tool

Open the PDF Tools Hub merge page in your browser. There is nothing to install and no account to create. The tool runs on your device, so your documents are not uploaded to a server to be merged.

Step 2: Add your PDF files

Click to add files, or drag them into the page. Add them in any order — you will arrange them in the next step. If you are merging a large number of files, add them in small batches so you can keep track of what is what.

Step 3: Arrange the files in order

Each file appears as a card. Drag the cards into the order you want the pages to appear in the final document. Read the filenames aloud as you go; it is easy to put two similar files the wrong way round. If you added a file by mistake, remove it here — do not leave it in and hope nobody notices.

Step 4: Merge and download

Click Merge. The tool joins the files in your chosen order and gives you one PDF to download. Open the downloaded file and scroll through it once, start to finish. Check that every page is present, in the right order, and that no page is blank or upside down. If something is wrong, go back and fix the order — do not send a file you have not checked.

When merging is the right answer — and when it is not

Merging is right when several documents belong together as one submission: a job application, a visa file, a school report, or a contract with attachments. It is not the right fix when the problem is file size — if the merged file is too large to upload, compress it afterwards instead of removing pages you need. And do not merge files just to hide a page; if a page does not belong in the submission, leave it out deliberately and honestly.

Tips for a clean result

  • Name files clearly before you merge. "01-application.pdf", "02-certificate.pdf" sorts correctly and prevents mix-ups.
  • Keep the merged filename simple, such as "application-documents.pdf". Avoid spaces and special characters — some upload forms reject them.
  • Check the total size. Many forms cap uploads at 5 MB or 10 MB. If your merged file is over the limit, compress it as a next step.
  • Keep your originals. Merging creates a new file; it does not delete the old ones. Keep them until the submission is accepted.
  • Open the result on your phone too. If the pages read correctly on a small screen, they will read correctly anywhere.

Frequently asked questions

Is merging PDFs really free?

Yes. The merge tool is free in your browser, with no account and no watermark on the result.

How many files can I merge at once?

There is no strict small limit for normal use. If you are combining dozens of large files, do it in two passes: merge half, then merge the halves.

Will merging change my pages?

No. Merging only joins pages in order. It does not change text, images, or layout. Each page keeps its original appearance.

Are my files uploaded anywhere?

No. The merge runs in your browser on your own device, so your documents stay with you.
